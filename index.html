What I took on

Turning three different needs into one roadmap

When I picked this up, buyers, agents and property teams were three separate problems with three separate wishlists. Nothing was sequenced, and nothing was built to work with anything else. My job was to turn that into one roadmap: decide what got built first, keep design and engineering pointed at the same release, and make sure the buyer experience, the agent workflow and the admin tooling actually fit together by the time each one shipped.

What follows is what came out of that: three connected surfaces, each one a piece of the same plan rather than a separate project.

  • What I ownedRoadmap, prioritization, release sequencing and cross-team coordination between design and engineering.
  • What shipped under my planOnboarding & auth, discovery & matching, saved lists, offers, leads, listing requests, and a full admin console.
  • Who it was built forBuyers and renters, independent agents, and property management teams.
  • Where it landedNative mobile apps for buyers and agents, plus a web console for teams.
Part one — what shipped for buyers

The buyer app

This is the flow I sequenced first: get someone signed in, get them to a match, and give them a reason to come back. Five screens, planned and delivered in this order.

01

Onboarding. Planned the sign-in flow — Apple, Google, or email in a couple of taps.

02

Sign in. Delivered a lighter return path for people already on the platform.

03

Home. Defined what earned a spot on the home screen — saved properties, offers, recent activity.

04

Property match. Scoped the details that had to be on this card — rooms, price, pets, parking.

05

Saved properties. Pushed for custom lists here instead of one long favorites feed.

Part two — what shipped for agents

The agent app

Agents needed to run their day from their phone. I prioritized these five flows so leads, listing requests and offers stayed in one tool instead of five.

01

Listing requests. Made sure every request carried the buyer's contact and what they were after.

02

Home. Brought leads, requests, inquiries, offers, properties and tasks into one view.

03

Offers. Set the stages every offer moves through, from connected to closed.

04

New offer. Cut this down to a short form agents can fill from a listing directly.

05

Inquiries. Queued booking requests with time and date so nothing falls through.

Part three — what shipped for the team

The admin console

The last piece was giving property teams one place to see the business — every lead, listing and offer, instead of piecing it together after the fact. This is the console I planned for them.

Decided what earned a place on the home screen

Leads, listing requests, inquiries, offers, properties and tasks — I made the call on which of these belonged on the first screen a team sees, with one-tap access to team management and property tools from there.

Made performance visible without a spreadsheet

I pushed for website traffic, conversion by device, and app downloads by user type to live on one screen — a straight answer to "how's it actually performing" instead of a request for a export.
